Hook formula library (first 15 seconds). Choose the formula that fits the topic's strongest fact, then write the
first line. A hook always does three things: opens a curiosity gap, makes the viewer care (money, status, fear of
being fooled), and promises a payoff inside this video.

1. number_stakes: lead with a surprising figure and what it means to the viewer.
   "A $300 cruise ticket is the cheapest thing you will pay for. Here is where the other $1,400 goes."
2. contradiction: say the opposite of what the viewer assumes.
   "The ship is not how this company makes money. The ship is the bait."
3. hidden_answer: ask a question the viewer cannot answer, then promise the answer.
   "Why does a coffee shop lose money on coffee and still get rich?"
4. cold_open: drop into a concrete moment, explain after.
   "It is 7 a.m. and the barista has already made the shop $4 before you taste anything."
5. villain_system: name a quiet mechanism working against the viewer.
   "There is a line on your bill that exists only to make the price look lower."
6. before_after: two numbers, same business, years apart.
   "In 2009 this airline earned $12 per passenger. Last year it earned $31. Nothing about the flight changed."
7. insider_view: put the viewer in the owner's chair.
   "You own a gym with 5,000 members. You need only 800 of them to show up. Here is why that is the plan."
8. challenge_guess: invite a guess, reveal later.
   "Guess how much of an airline's profit comes from selling seats. Hold that number, because it is wrong."

Hook rules: first sentence 12 words or fewer; no greeting, no channel name, no "in this video"; one idea only;
say "by the end" or an equivalent promise; never promise something the video does not deliver.

Curiosity devices to reuse later (each creates a reason to keep watching):
- open loop: "The third one is the one nobody notices." (pay it off 1-3 minutes later)
- numbered countdown: "Three places the money hides" then deliver 1, 2, 3.
- stakes reminder: "Keep that $1,400 in mind."
- reversal: "That sounds like the answer. It is not."
- zoom-out: show the same number at company scale.
- mini-cliffhanger: end a segment on the question the next one answers.
